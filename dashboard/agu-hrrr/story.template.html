The motivation

Helene raised
the question.

After Hurricane Helene hit the Appalachians in September 2024, we wanted to know how much local information could help a national river forecast.

The study tests an earlier period. We lacked matching native NWM forecast leads for Helene, so the storm contributes no cases to the results below.

Research design

Three gauges.
One forecast clock.

At Jefferson, Galax and Sugar Grove, each model learns a local adjustment to the NWM flow for each of the next 18 hours. All comparisons use matching forecasts and accepted gauge observations.

How archive records become training cases

We matched each gauge with its NWM reach and its basin weather archive. We preserved issue times, native leads, units, source versions and quality masks. Weather comes from the latest admitted HRRR cycle at or before the NWM issue. Each forecast field must match the target hour. A missing lead stays missing.

Each site uses its own scales fitted on the training window. History, forecast support and observed targets stay inside their assigned interval. Paired models and input removals use the same eligible cases. Historical publication times and gauge revisions remain uncertain.

Model design

Three ways to learn a correction

Every network adds a residual to NWM. The three families differ in how they represent history and the hours ahead. Each shares one training recipe across sites and learns separate weights at each gauge.

LSTM

A recurrent encoder summarizes the past week. A decoder carries that memory from one forecast hour to the next.

AdamW LR 0.0003 · up to 200 epochs

GRU + convolution

A gated recurrent network carries streamflow history forward. Temporal convolutions combine information across neighboring forecast hours.

AdamW LR 0.0001 · up to 100 epochs

Temporal Transformer

Attention connects three-hour patches of gauge history with forecast-hour tokens and a separate soil-state token.

AdamW LR 0.0003 · up to 100 epochs

How we separated training and evaluation

  1. May 2021 – Jan 2022

    Fit

    Fit local weights and scales.

  2. Feb – Apr 2022

    Validate

    Select the trained epoch. Refit on the full first year for that many epochs.

  3. May 2022 – Apr 2023

    Evaluate

    Score frozen forecasts on matching cases. Earlier studies had used this year.

Three gauges × three families × three seeds × nine input sets yielded 243 development models and 243 first-year refits. Checkpoints minimized pooled normalized squared error on validation with patience 10. Evaluation used RMSE, MAE, original Kling–Gupta efficiency, Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency and Pearson correlation. Signed and percent bias were secondary diagnostics.

Discussion

What carries
the forecast?

The answer depends on the gauge, the lead and which error matters.

Explore all input removals ↗

Soil moisture mattered most where correction worked.

Removing it and retraining raised pooled squared error by @@SOIL_J@@ at Jefferson and @@SOIL_G@@ at Galax. The experiments measure predictive contributions under a fixed recipe. They cannot isolate a physical cause.

Simple forecasts set a demanding benchmark.

At Jefferson, all three intervals comparing networks with a lead-specific linear regression included zero. At Galax, persistence retained a higher Kling–Gupta efficiency at every lead.

Sugar Grove sets a limit on the claim.

The three networks stayed within about 1% of NWM’s pooled squared error. At H1, the Transformer’s MAE was @@GROVE_MODEL_H1@@ m³/s, compared with @@GROVE_NWM_H1@@ m³/s for NWM. The evidence supports local gains at two sites and leaves their hydrologic cause open.

One November week shaped the year.

The week starting 8 November 2022 supplied 45–67% of NWM squared error across the gauges. They share storms, so independent flood-event skill remains open. Training used NWM v2.1, while v2.2 supplied 85% of scored cells. The pointwise week-block intervals hold the three fitted seeds fixed and undercovered in calibration.
